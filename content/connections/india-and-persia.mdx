## Persia's Indian provinces

Under Darius I, the Achaemenid Persian empire reached the Indus. Persian royal inscriptions name
the Indian provinces, and Greek writers report the tribute they paid
<Cite id="briant-2002" /> <Cite id="godley-1921" />. Persian control seems to have faded before
Alexander's arrival in the 320s BCE <Cite id="briant-2002" />.

## Scripts and stone

Aramaic, the Persian empire's working script, left a lasting mark: the Kharoshthi script of the
north-west grew out of it <Cite id="singh-2008" />. Some historians also see Persian influence in
the polished stone and animal capitals of Ashoka's pillars, while others stress their Indian roots
<Cite id="thapar-2002" />.

## A game that travelled

Centuries later, under the Sasanian dynasty, the Indian game chaturanga travelled to Persia and
became chatrang, the game that later passed to the Arab world and Europe as chess
<Cite id="murray-1913" />. See [Inventions & ideas](/ideas).
