## The old picture: "Indianized states"

In the mid-twentieth century, George Cœdès described the early kingdoms of Southeast Asia as
"Indianized states", shaped by Indian traders, priests and settlers <Cite id="coedes-1968" />.
His work gathered a huge range of inscriptions and remains a starting point.

## The newer picture: local choice

Later historians, notably O. W. Wolters, argued that Southeast Asian rulers chose Indian ideas
and reshaped them for their own purposes, a process he called "localization"
<Cite id="wolters-1982" />. On this view, Angkor and Borobudur are Southeast Asian achievements
that drew on Indian traditions, not Indian monuments abroad.

## The Chola expedition

Around 1025, the Chola king Rajendra I attacked Srivijaya. Historians still debate whether it was
a raid for plunder and prestige or an attempt to protect Chola trade with China
<Cite id="kulke-2009" />.
