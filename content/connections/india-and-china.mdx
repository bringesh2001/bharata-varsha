## A religion crosses the mountains

Buddhism reached China in the first centuries CE along the trade routes of Central Asia. Monks,
many from the Kushan lands, began translating Buddhist texts into Chinese, a project that went on
for centuries <Cite id="zurcher-1959" />.

## Pilgrims as historians

Chinese pilgrims came to India to study and to collect texts. Faxian in the early 400s and
Xuanzang in the 600s left detailed accounts of the places they visited, including Pataliputra,
Nalanda and many monasteries <Cite id="legge-1886" /> <Cite id="beal-1884" />. Historians of India
rely on them heavily, while remembering that they saw India through Buddhist eyes.

## From pilgrims to merchants

From about the 600s, Tansen Sen argues, the relationship shifted from Buddhist exchange towards
diplomacy and maritime trade, with south Indian kingdoms such as the Cholas sending missions to
China <Cite id="sen-2003" />.
