## Where the argument came from

The drain argument was not invented by later historians. It was made by Indians living under
colonial rule. Dadabhai Naoroji collected his papers on Indian poverty in a book published in
1901 <Cite id="naoroji-1901" />, and R. C. Dutt wrote a detailed economic history the following
year <Cite id="dutt-1902" />. Their work became a foundation of Indian economic nationalism
<Cite id="chandra-1966" />.

## The challenge

In 1963 the American economic historian Morris D. Morris argued that the nationalist account
overstated the damage done by British policy <Cite id="morris-1963" />. Other historians
replied at length, and the questions he raised still shape how the period is studied.

## A note on labels

This debate does not split neatly into "Indian" and "western" sides. Some of the most
influential arguments on both sides come from Indian scholars. That is why we label each view by
what it argues <Cite id="roy-2000" />.
