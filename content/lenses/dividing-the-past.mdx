## Where the scheme came from

James Mill's *History of British India* (1817) divided India's past into Hindu, Muslim and
British periods <Cite id="mill-1817" />. The scheme was simple and widely copied in textbooks.

## What historians use instead

Romila Thapar and others replaced it with periods such as ancient, early medieval and medieval,
built around changes in economy and society as well as politics <Cite id="thapar-2002" />.
Richard Eaton describes the centuries after 1000 as a "Persianate age", stressing a shared
culture rather than a religious divide <Cite id="eaton-2019" />.

## What this site does

Bharata Varsha organises history by twelve eras, each named after its main states or features,
and groups them as ancient, classical, medieval and modern only for colour coding. The aim is to
describe each period on its own terms.
