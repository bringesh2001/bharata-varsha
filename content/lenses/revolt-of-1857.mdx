## A label is an argument

What we call 1857 already takes a side. "Mutiny" puts the soldiers' disobedience at the centre.
"War of independence" puts the nation at the centre. V. D. Savarkar's book of 1909, banned in
British India, made the second label famous <Cite id="savarkar-1909" />.

## Indian historians disagreed too

This debate is a good example of why we label views by their argument and not by nationality.
In the centenary year of 1957, two Indian historians reached different conclusions. Surendra Nath
Sen's official history concluded that what began as a fight for religion ended as a war of
independence, though not a national one <Cite id="sen-1957" />. R. C. Majumdar, a strongly
nationalist historian, argued that it was neither the first nor a national war of independence
<Cite id="majumdar-1957" />.

## History from the villages

Later work moved away from the label to ask who rose and why. Studies of Awadh and of individual
districts show peasants and landholders joining in large numbers, often for reasons rooted in
land, revenue and the loss of their rulers <Cite id="mukherjee-1984" /> <Cite id="stokes-1986" />.
